Off-the-shelf CRMs are a fine default. They ship in a week, they cover the 80% cases, and they have a marketplace of integrations. The reason we still write custom CRMs is not because we don't like HubSpot it's because there's a real crossover point where a custom system wins on cost, on process fit, and on speed of change.

## The three signals it's time to look at custom

**1. License bloat has passed six figures.**
When per-seat licensing crosses roughly $50k–$150k per year, the maths on a custom build usually starts to work. That's before you add the "professional services" hours you spend keeping the standard tool aligned with your process.

**2. Configuration is your full-time job.**
If a person on your team spends half their week configuring the CRM to stay in sync with how the business actually sells, you've already crossed over. You're paying for a platform *and* paying to keep it usable.

**3. Reporting is behind a wall.**
If the reports you actually need require an enterprise tier or a paid consultant you're renting insight into your own business.

## What a custom CRM should include on day one

- **A data model that fits your reality.** Accounts, contacts, deals, activities but with your fields, your enums, your rules. No made-up abstractions.
- **Integrations to your billing, marketing, and support.** Two-way, tested, with clear error paths.
- **Role-based access.** So the audit conversation is short.
- **A reporting surface you own.** Not a report *builder* the actual reports your leadership already asks for.
- **Documentation and tests handed over.** So the second engineer who touches this can move in a day, not a quarter.

## What a custom CRM shouldn't try to be

It shouldn't be a marketing automation platform. It shouldn't be your data warehouse. It shouldn't be your ticketing system. The wins come from the thing being small, focused, and well-integrated not from re-inventing three adjacent categories at once.

## A pragmatic 6-month plan

Weeks 1–2: interviews, current-tool audit, data-model draft.
Weeks 3–4: interactive prototype of the top three workflows.
Weeks 5–12: incremental sprints, live for a friendly internal group from week 6.
Weeks 13–20: migration, training, cutover, hypercare.
Weeks 21–24: measurement + trim the fat.

The result should feel small and personal. If it feels heavy, we built the wrong thing.
