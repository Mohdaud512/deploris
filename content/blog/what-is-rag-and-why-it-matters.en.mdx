If you've heard the acronym RAG and nodded along, you're not alone. It stands for **retrieval-augmented generation**, and it's the pattern behind most trustworthy business AI shipping today.

## The one-paragraph version

A retriever finds the passages in your knowledge base that are most relevant to a question. A language model then answers using those passages and only those passages and cites the source. The user gets an answer they can verify; the business gets a system whose facts they can audit.

## Why not just fine-tune a model?

Fine-tuning changes the model's weights. That's expensive to run, hard to update, and impossible to audit at the fact level. RAG leaves the model as-is and just feeds it your current documents. When you update a doc, the next answer reflects it no retraining.

## Where RAG wins

- **Support and internal knowledge.** Employees or customers keep asking the same knowledge-heavy questions.
- **Regulated content.** You need to prove which source drove a claim.
- **Docs that change often.** Product notes, policies, release notes.

## Where RAG struggles

- **Genuinely novel synthesis.** If the answer isn't in your corpus, a RAG system still won't invent it (which is a *feature*, but sometimes an inconvenience).
- **Highly conversational tasks with no source.** Small-talk, negotiation, unstructured brainstorming.

## The five things a production RAG needs

1. **A curated corpus.** Not "everything on SharePoint" the sources people trust.
2. **Retrieval evaluation.** Measured precision, recall, and answer accuracy on a real question set that grows over time.
3. **Access control.** The retriever must respect existing permissions, not sidestep them.
4. **Source citation in every answer.** No exceptions.
5. **A monitoring surface.** So you know when quality drifts.
